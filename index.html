The Definition

Agents Act. Chatbots Answer.

An agent perceives, plans, uses tools and acts — then checks the result and iterates.

Definition Perceive → plan → act → reflect

  • Agentic AI — “autonomous systems perceive their environment, plan multi-step action sequences, and execute those plans with minimal human intervention.” (Databricks, 2026)
  • A chatbot's job ends at the answer. An agent's job ends at the outcome — a chiller adjusted, a battery re-dispatched, a plume flagged.
  • There is still no single agreed definition (BetaNews, 2026) — so this deck fixes one, here, and sticks to it. The human sets the goal and the guardrails.
Sources: Databricks, “Agentic AI vs Generative AI” (updated Nov 2026) · BetaNews, “What Is Agentic AI?” (2026) — links on slide 14.

Why Now

Climate Work Is a Loop

Grids, buildings, farms and forests don't wait for a prompt. Three enablers make continuous action practical.

Enablers Three things changed

  • Continuous sensing — ~1,300 wildfire cameras in California feed AI detection around the clock (CAL FIRE, via AP, May 2026).
  • Connected tools — APIs, building controls and grid dispatch an agent can call, inside bounds set in advance.
  • An always-on loop — sense → decide → act → re-sense every few minutes, not every meeting.

Cluster 1 · See Sooner

Forecast First, Respond Early

The clearest wins in climate AI are seeing sooner — floods and fire, days or hours before they hit.

Early Warning Verified deployments

  • Google Flood Hub — riverine flood forecasts up to 7 days ahead, covering 100 countries and 700M people (Google, updated Jan 2026).
  • ALERTCalifornia — ~1,300 cameras; in 2025 it flagged fires before any 911 call >50% of the time (AP, May 2026).
  • Humans stay in charge — dispatchers review every camera alert before engines roll; fog, dust and steam cause false positives (AP, May 2026).

Cluster 2 · Run Cleaner

Optimise What Already Exists

Agents earn their keep squeezing waste out of buildings, grids and traffic — inside bounds a human sets.

Energy Bounded optimisation

  • Bounded optimisation — the agent tunes setpoints only within pre-set ranges, and a human or safety controller can overrule it.
  • Google Green Light (modelled, not measured): up to −30% stops, −10% emissions at intersections in 100+ cities — city engineers approve each change (Google Research, 2026).
  • Label the evidence — “up to” and “modelled” are not measured savings. Demand a baseline before believing a percentage.
Source: Google Research, Project Green Light (2026) — slide 14.

Cluster 3 · In the Field

Precision — and a Warning

A field agent sprays only the weeds it sees. Its failure mode teaches the guardrail lesson of this whole talk.

Field Targeted action

  • Targeted action — camera-guided sprayers treat only the weeds they detect, not the whole field.
  • The warning (Avent et al., Weed Technology, 2026): in a 3-year Arkansas trial (prototype machine, single site), low-sensitivity settings saved herbicide but let the Palmer amaranth population and soil seed bank grow +280% a year.
  • The lesson — savings and weed control are the same dial. Specify the objective in climate terms, not task terms.
Source: Avent et al., Weed Technology (2026) — slide 14.
+280% / yrPalmer amaranth population & seed bank growth under low-sensitivity settings (2026 trial)
Mis-specified objective → resistant weeds. The agent did exactly what it was told.
Cluster 4 · Prove It

Measure Emissions Independently

Self-reported inventories understate reality. Satellites plus AI are becoming the audit layer — but detection is not abatement.

MRV Observation, then action

  • Climate TRACE — an independent coalition's facility-level emissions inventory, built from satellite data + AI, updated monthly.
  • MethaneSAT (preprint, May 2026 — not yet peer-reviewed): across 34 regions, satellite estimates run ~60% above bottom-up inventories.
  • The gap: only ~12% of satellite methane alerts received an operator response (IEA Global Methane Tracker, 2026). An agent that only alerts is not a climate solution.
Sources: Climate TRACE · MethaneSAT preprint (2026) · IEA Global Methane Tracker (2026) — slide 14.

The Other Ledger

AI Has a Footprint Too

Every benefit in this talk must be netted against the energy the AI itself uses. The 2026 IEA update, in three points:

Footprint IEA, April 2026

  • 485 TWh — data-centre electricity in 2025, up 17% in a single year (IEA, Apr 2026).
  • ~950 TWh by 2030 — demand roughly doubles in five years (IEA, Apr 2026).
  • The rebound: energy per AI task is falling ~10× a year, yet totals still rise — efficiency alone will not cap the footprint.

Responsible Deployment

Three Guardrails, Non-Negotiable

No agent touches a physical system without all three. This pattern sits behind every success story in this talk.

Guardrails The rule

  • 1 · Bounded action space — the agent may only do pre-approved things, inside pre-set ranges.
  • 2 · Human or independent veto — wildfire dispatchers review every camera alert before anything rolls (AP, 2026); a human can halt the agent at any time.
  • 3 · Audit log & kill-switch — every tool call recorded, replayable, attributable — and the agent can be frozen on demand.
Source: the deployment pattern across the 2026 cases in this deck (AP, May 2026) — slide 14.

Failure Modes

What Can Go Wrong

Agents fail by acting, not by answering. The 2026 evidence, in three points:

Risks Evidence first

  • OWASP Agentic Top 10 (2026): #1 goal hijack — text the agent reads replaces its objective; #2 tool misuse; #3 identity & privilege abuse.
  • UNESCO / UCL (Nov 2026): models averaged 33.8% — chance level (33.3%) — when combining two climate policies; under pushback, every one of the 15 models tested eventually abandoned a correct climate fact.
  • Drift: no single step fails, yet the outcome wanders from the intent — the Palmer amaranth case (Avent et al., 2026; slide 6).
Sources: OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications (2026) · UNESCO / UCL, “Can We Trust AI on Climate?”, Nov 2026 — slide 14.

The Checklist

Before You Deploy

Three checks that separate climate action from climate marketing.

Avoid Do not ship if…

  • The % has no source, date or baseline — 74% of 154 AI net-climate-benefit statements, drawn from 8 sources incl. the IEA, Google and Microsoft, were unproven (Joshi report, Feb 2026).
  • The benefit is gross, not net of the AI's own energy and water (IEA, Apr 2026; slide 8).
  • The agent alerts but nobody owns the response — the 12% methane-alert lesson (IEA, 2026; slide 7).

Synthesis

Potential Is Real — If Directed

A 2026 critique shows the biggest savings claims lean on optimistic adoption assumptions. Potential is not destiny: policy and design decide.

Call to Action Three asks

  • 1 · Fund closed loops — detect → act → verify — not demos that only alert.
  • 2 · Mandate measurement — every climate agent reports kWh, CO₂e and litres next to its benefit, per task.
  • 3 · Guardrails before autonomy — bounds, veto, log, kill-switch, in physical systems, first.
One line to remember: “Agents should earn autonomy — in small, bounded, verified steps.”
Source: Walton et al., “Modelling diverse futures of AI and the climate”, preprint, Feb 2026 — slide 14.
