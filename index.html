The Definition

Agents Act. Chatbots Answer.

One definition for the whole talk: an agent perceives, plans, uses tools and acts — then checks the result and iterates.

Definition Perceive → plan → act → reflect

  • Agentic AI — “autonomous systems perceive their environment, plan multi-step action sequences, and execute those plans with minimal human intervention.” (Databricks, 2026)
  • A chatbot's job ends at the answer. An agent's job ends at the outcome — a chiller adjusted, a battery re-dispatched, a plume flagged.
  • The human sets the goal and the guardrails — not every step in between.
  • There is still no single agreed definition (BetaNews, 2026) — so this deck fixes one, here, and sticks to it.
Sources: Databricks, “Agentic AI vs Generative AI” (updated Nov 2026) · Neo4j (2025) · BetaNews, “What Is Agentic AI?” (2026) — full links on slide 14.

Why Now

Climate Work Is a Loop

Grids, buildings, farms and forests don't wait for a prompt. They need systems that run continuously — and three enablers just made that practical.

Enablers Three things changed

  • Cheap, continuous sensing — ~1,300 wildfire cameras in California; satellites scanning for methane; 7 million smart water meters at SUEZ alone.
  • Forecast models — GraphCast produces a 10-day global weather forecast in under a minute (DeepMind, Science, 2023).
  • Tools an agent can call — APIs, building controls, grid dispatch — with bounds on what the agent may touch.
  • UN framing (TEC, July 2025): AI can optimise grids, forecast demand and extreme weather, and support biodiversity — if governance and data keep up.
Sources: UN Technology Executive Committee paper, July 2025 (via Global Government Forum) · DeepMind GraphCast, 2023 — slide 14.

Cluster 1 · See Sooner

Forecast First, Respond Early

The best-evidenced wins in climate AI are seeing sooner — floods, fire and weather, days or hours before they hit.

Early Warning Verified deployments

  • Google Flood Hub — riverine flood forecasts up to 7 days ahead; expanded Nov 2024 to 100 countries, 700M people (Nature, Mar 2024).
  • ALERTCalifornia — ~1,300 cameras; in 2025 it flagged fires before any 911 call >50% of the time; the Trotter Fire was held to 52 acres vs ~4,000 projected (CAL FIRE data).
  • GraphCast — beat ECMWF's flagship forecast on >90% of 1,380 test variables (DeepMind, Science, 2023).
  • Context: early warning systems in general cut flood fatalities by up to 43% — a general figure, not Flood-Hub-specific (ICTWorks, 2024).
Sources: Google Flood Hub blog (2024, updated Jan 2026) · AP wildfire report, 1 May 2026 · DeepMind (2023) — slide 14.

Cluster 2 · Run Cleaner

Optimise What Already Exists

Agents earn their keep squeezing waste out of grids, buildings and traffic. Every figure below is labelled by who reported it.

Energy Four deployments

  • DeepMind wind (company-reported): +20% value from 700 MW of wind, forecasting output 36 hours ahead.
  • DeepMind cooling (company-reported, 2016): −40% cooling energy in Google data centres; from 2018 it set cooling directly every 5 minutes, inside safety bounds.
  • BrainBox AI HVAC (vendor-reported): up to −25% energy and −40% GHG in commercial buildings (Trane Technologies).
  • Google Green Light (modelled, not measured): up to −30% stops, −10% emissions at intersections in 100+ cities — city engineers approve each change.

Cluster 3 · Grow & Protect

Land, Water, Wildlife

The clearest “agent in the field” sprays only the weeds it sees — and its failure mode teaches the guardrail lesson of this whole talk.

Field Three deployments

  • John Deere See & Spray — cameras decide in ≤200 ms and spray only weeds; an Iowa State demo (415 acres, 2024) saved 76% of herbicide on average (43.9–90.6% per field).
  • SUEZ Smart Water (company report, Dec 2025): AI leak detection saved 33M m³ of water in 2024 — 8,900 Olympic pools — across 7M connected meters.
  • Wildlife Insights / SpeciesNet — AI sorts camera traps: SpeciesNet trained on 65M+ images, 1,295 species; up to 80% of trap images are blanks it filters out.
The warning: in a 3-year University of Arkansas trial, See & Spray's low-sensitivity settings saved herbicide but let Palmer amaranth grow +280%/yr. Savings and weed control are the same dial — specify the objective in climate terms, not task terms.
Sources: Agrifood AI Field Guide (2026) incl. Arkansas trial · SUEZ (2025) · FruitPunch AI / Gray Group — slide 14.

Cluster 4 · Prove It

Measure Emissions Independently

Self-reported inventories understate reality. Satellites plus AI are becoming the audit layer — but detection is not abatement.

MRV Observation, then action

  • Climate TRACE — an independent coalition's facility-level emissions inventory, built from satellite data + AI, updated monthly.
  • MethaneSAT (preprint, May 2026 — not yet peer-reviewed): across 34 regions, satellite estimates run ~60% above bottom-up inventories.
  • Carbon Mapper Tanager-1 — a Texas plume of 7,100 kg/h was reported, repaired, and re-observed clean 15 days later: the closed loop, documented.
  • The gap: only ~12% of UNEP MARS satellite alerts received an operator response in 2025. An agent that only alerts is not a climate solution.
Sources: Climate TRACE · MethaneSAT preprint (Research Square, 2026) · Carbon Mapper via Vrulum (2026) — slide 14. MethaneSAT lost contact in June 2025; MRV continuity is itself a risk.

The Other Ledger

AI Has a Footprint Too

Every benefit in this talk must be netted against the energy and water the AI itself uses. Green AI starts with honest accounting.

Footprint The numbers

  • Training GPT-3: 552 tCO₂e. The same-size BLOOM model on France's cleaner grid: 24.7 t. Where you compute matters more than the model (Patterson et al., 2021).
  • One median Gemini prompt: 0.24 Wh (Google, Aug 2025 — not independently verified). Tiny per prompt; agentic loops multiply calls by orders of magnitude.
  • Water (US, 2023): data centres drank 66bn litres on site — and ~800bn more at the power plants behind them (LBNL, 2024).
  • Mitigation that works: smaller / sparse models, clean grids, carbon-aware scheduling, capped agent loops — and measurement per task.
Vintage warning: the IEA's live page (Nov 2026) shows a revised series — 460 TWh (2024) → >1,000 TWh (2030). The chart uses the April 2025 report. One vintage per slide; never mix them.

Responsible Deployment

Four Guardrails, Non-Negotiable

No agent touches a physical system without all four. This pattern sits behind every success story in this talk.

Guardrails The rule

  • 1 · Bounded action space — the agent may only do pre-approved things, inside pre-set ranges.
  • 2 · Human or independent veto — DeepMind cooling: the site's own control system checks every action before it applies, every 5 minutes.
  • 3 · Audit log — every tool call recorded, replayable, attributable. Green Light: engineers accept or reject each suggestion.
  • 4 · Kill-switch & safe stop — wildfire dispatchers review camera alerts before anything rolls; a human can halt the agent at any time.
Sources: DeepMind cooling case synthesis · Google Green Light · AP wildfire report (2026) — slide 14. The same through-line runs through NIST AI RMF, the EU AI Act and OWASP's “least-agency” principle.

Governance

Frameworks That Apply

Use the combination — values, method, audit, law — not any one framework on its own.

Frameworks The stack

  • OECD AI Principles (updated May 2024) — the values: human-centred, transparent, accountable. Endorsed by 47 countries.
  • NIST AI RMF (Jan 2023) — the method: Govern, Map, Measure, Manage.
  • ISO/IEC 42001 (Dec 2023) — the audit: a certifiable AI management system a buyer can check.
  • EU AI Act (in force Aug 2024) — the law: risk tiers; high-risk duties phase in from 2026, some deferred to 2027–28.
  • UN TEC (July 2025) — climate-specific: build data and skills, design for inclusion, manage AI's own energy and water.
Sources: OECD · NIST · ISO · Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 · UN TEC (2025) — links on slide 14.

Failure Modes

What Can Go Wrong

Agents fail by acting, not by answering. These are documented failure modes — with evidence, not hypotheticals.

Risks Evidence first

  • OWASP Agentic AI Top 10 (2026): #1 goal hijack — text the agent reads replaces its objective; #2 tool misuse; #3 identity & privilege abuse.
  • UNESCO / UCL (Nov 2026): 33 models scored 33.8% — chance level — when combining two climate policies; under pushback, every model tested eventually abandoned a correct climate fact.
  • Gartner (forecast, June 2025): >40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by end-2027 — escalating costs, unclear value, inadequate risk controls.
  • Drift: no single step fails, yet the outcome wanders from the intent — the See & Spray resistance case (slide 6).
Sources: OWASP Agentic AI Top 10 (released 9 Dec 2025) · UNESCO, “Can We Trust AI on Climate?”, COP31, 16 Nov 2026 · Gartner press release, 25 June 2025 — slide 14.

The Checklist

Before You Deploy

Five checks that separate climate action from climate marketing.

Avoid Do not ship if…

  • The % has no source, date or baseline — 74% of 154 corporate AI-climate claims lacked verifiable evidence (Joshi report, Feb 2026).
  • The benefit is gross, not net of the AI's own energy and water (slide 8).
  • The agent alerts but nobody owns the response — the 12% MARS lesson (slide 7).
  • The model was never validated on local data, language or conditions — the UN TEC warning for developing countries (2025).
  • Autonomy is open-ended in a physical system — no bounds, no veto, no log (slide 9).
Sources: Ketan Joshi, “AI-climate claims” report (Feb 2026, via The Outpost) · UN TEC (2025) · UNEP MARS / IEA — slide 14.

Synthesis

Potential Is Real — If Directed

Peer-reviewed potential: AI could cut 3.2–5.4 GtCO₂e a year by 2035 in power, food and transport — against its own 0.4–1.6 Gt cost. Potential, not destiny: policy and design decide.

Call to Action Three asks

  • 1 · Fund closed loops — detect → act → verify — not demos that only alert.
  • 2 · Mandate measurement — every climate agent reports kWh, CO₂e and litres next to its benefit, per task.
  • 3 · Guardrails before autonomy — bounds, veto, log, kill-switch, in physical systems, first.
One line to remember: “Agents should earn autonomy — in small, bounded, verified steps.”
